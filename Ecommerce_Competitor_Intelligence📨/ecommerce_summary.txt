---

### 1. Price Disparity Highlights  
- **Premium Positioning (NK‑01, NK‑03):** We are pricing 12‑20 % above the direct competitor on two core items, which could pressure price‑sensitive shoppers, especially on a high‑volume, performance‑driven category (running shoes).  
- **Undercut (NK‑02):** The hoodie is priced 7‑8 % lower than the competitor, giving us a clear price advantage, but the low‑stock signal may blunt the benefit.  
- **Stock‑Driven Gaps:** The most pronounced premium (NK‑03) coincides with an out‑of‑stock situation, potentially magnifying lost sales and brand perception of unavailability.

---

### 2. Strategic Positioning Risks  
| Risk | Why It Matters | SKU(s) Affected |
|------|----------------|-----------------|
| **Price‑sensitive churn** | A $15 premium on a high‑interest item (running shoes) can push shoppers to the lower‑priced alternative, especially when the competitor’s price is within the “sweet‑spot” range for this segment. | NK‑01 |
| **Perceived over‑pricing** | A 20 % premium on compression shorts (NK‑03) may signal a premium quality claim we are not substantiating (e.g., limited features, brand cachet). Coupled with out‑of‑stock, this can erode trust. | NK‑03 |
| **Margin vs. volume trade‑off** | Under‑pricing the hoodie (NK‑02) protects market share but squeezes margin. If stock remains low, the price advantage cannot be fully leveraged, and we risk stock‑outs that frustrate price‑sensitive buyers. | NK‑02 |
| **Stock‑price mismatch** | Low stock on a lower‑priced item (NK‑02) creates a “scarcity‑premium” paradox—customers see a good deal but can’t buy it, potentially driving them to the competitor despite the price edge. | NK‑02 |
| **Brand equity dilution** | Maintaining a premium price on out‑of‑stock items (NK‑03) may signal poor inventory planning, harming the brand’s reputation for reliability. | NK‑03 |
